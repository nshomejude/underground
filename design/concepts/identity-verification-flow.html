        Decision

        Design & compliance notes

        Real-world requirements an implementation must meet. This file is a visual concept only.

        • Explicit, unbundled consent before any capture, with a written retention schedule and purpose. Consent must be as easy to withdraw as to give. Log consent version and timestamp.
        • Biometric law: treat face data as special-category (GDPR Art. 9) and as a biometric identifier (Illinois BIPA, Texas CUBI, similar). Provide a public retention and destruction policy, written release where required, and a DPIA.
        • No storage of raw biometrics unless strictly required. Delete images and templates after the check; keep only result, timestamp and reason code.
        • Use a certified provider (for example ISO/IEC 30107-3 PAD tested, NIST FRVT evaluated, iBeta level 2) instead of self-built matching. Sign a DPA and confirm data residency and sub-processors.
        • Accessibility alternative path: a manual route for users who cannot or will not use a camera, with equal outcomes and no penalty. Offer sufficient time, screen-reader labels, text alternatives for every animation, and reduced-motion support.
        • Human review for declines. No solely automated adverse decision (GDPR Art. 22): show reason codes, offer appeal, and a named reviewer path within a stated time.
        • Security: encrypt in transit and at rest, short-lived signed sessions, audit logs, rate limits, no biometric data in analytics or URLs.
